Dear Sir,



We, the Student Council, wish to respond to your university wide mail that
is announcing that there is in fact no health “crisis” or concerns
regarding pollution in our university. The Student Council does not agree
with this statement and our disagreement is on the basis of AQI reports
from different sources that we have observed over the last few days which
we have attached below. Almost all reports show a range of *300-500 AQI *within
the Dadri area and our campus exactly. In addition, *construction
activities on campus are further adding to the pollution levels within the
area*, as well as *crop burning* happening near the boundaries of our
campus. We have attached video proofs of both for your reference.



We have already communicated the concerns of both the student body and
faculty alike yesterday to you and the Dean of Academics along with
proposals for possible solutions that can be deliberated upon. *Having
already stressed the urgency of the situation in our original mail, we find
ourselves unsure of why we have not received any response or
acknowledgement from the administration*.

Taking into account the fact that the mail sent out by the DSA has gone out
to all students and faculty, we as the Student Council are obliged to
inform all students and faculty of the recommendations that we have
actually made to the administration:

   1. Allowing for professors to conduct online classes per their
   discretion and discussion with their class. While we are not demanding a
   complete switch to online classes, we request that a central instruction go
   out informing professors that if they deem it necessary then they can
   choose to hold classes online.
   2. Shifting early morning classes, which are held from 8-10:30 AM, to a
   later slot in order to avoid students going out during peak smog hours.
   3. Repealing the new exam rules that were put in place during the
   mid-semester exams. The new system has forced students to be in a situation
   where they must stand in long queues up to 15 minutes before their exams.
   Considering the present weather and pollution conditions, this must be
   reconsidered immediately. Students should not be forced to wait to give
   their exams.
   4. Installation of air purifiers in all classrooms and major buildings
   such as the library. With the winters here, the ACs are no longer being
   used and resources can now be diverted into ensuring that the most occupied
   spaces within campus are safe to breathe in for students and staff alike.
   5. Provision of N-95 masks to all students and staff. These can be made
   available at all security desks in the Academic Blocks, G-Block and hostel
   receptions. This will encourage students and staff alike to be mindful of
   their health and make masks accessible for all.
   6. A temporary stop to all construction activity on campus. Current
   construction work is only adding to the pollution levels on campus. We have
   attached below two videos displaying the amount of dust clouds that are
   being generated at the football field alone, yesterday evening, right next
   to the Fourth and Fifth Hostel clusters. Making it impossible to access the
   area unless one wants to risk their health.



With AQI levels as high as they are, the health of all students and faculty
should be a primary concern for the university yet it is ignoring its
responsibility to its primary stakeholders. We urge for immediate actions
to be taken upon proper consultation with the student body and faculty. *If
students and faculty wish to show their support towards this endeavour, as
well as offer suggestions, we urge you all to respond to this mail trail.
Let us raise our concerns collectively. *

Thanks and Regards,
