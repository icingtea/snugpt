Academic Collaborations
Students of BA (Research) at Shiv Nadar IoE can study for one semester at an
overseas university and transfer the earned credits to the program they are pursuing.
The placements in host institutions are facilitated by the International Office of the
University.
Students of the Department have successfully completed exchange semesters at the
universities of Warwick (UK) and Bologna (Italy), and Nanyang Technological
University (Singapore). Additionally, the Department hosts academic partnerships,
also through the MoU’s of the University: with universities of Michigan and
Pennsylvania (USA), Universitat Pompeu Fabra (Barcelona, Spain), The French
National Research Institute for Sustainable Development (IRD, Paris) and University
of Brighton (U.K).
Internships
Our students have interned with renown academic and cultural organizations such as: Indian National
Trust for Art and Cultural Heritage (INTACH), The Habitats Trust, Eka Resources, The Kala Chaupal Trust,
Tibet Museum (Dharamshala), Indira Gandhi Rashtriya Manav Sangrahalaya (Bhopal), Maharaja Sawai

Committee (New Delhi) and India Fellow Program (Bengaluru). They have participated in the
archaeological project at Mandu (M.P.), and a new collaborative Maski Archaeological Research Project
(Maski, Karnataka) promises a future site for field-studies. Moreover, the Department anticipates new
internship opportunities in museums and visual arts, and in education, development and livelihoods
sectors.
The work experience and practical skills acquired through the internships have facilitated well-placed
jobs and admissions into higher education programmes, particularly in universities outside India.
Opportunities for Undergraduate
Research (OUR Program)
Students start their research journeys early in Shiv Nadar IoE. Classroom projects
rapidly lead to deeper academic quests, and the University’s pioneering OUR
program provide funding and faculty mentoring for promising pilot studies.
Over the years, our students have produced many good research papers through
the OUR scheme. The versatility of the research is represented through topics
such as: ‘An Archaeological Ethnography of the Tibetan Community in India’,
‘Rock Art and Archaeology: Analysing Space and Meaning in Adamgarh’, ‘Curato-
rial Practices at the Hill Palace Museum, Cochin’, ‘Medieval Delhi and the
Patterning of the Domestic Space’, ‘Effects of Territorialisation on Census Data in
Madras Presidency and Mysuru’, ‘A Palaeobotanical Study from Rohana Khurd,
Uttar Pradesh, c. 4th Century BCE – 10th Century CE’, ‘Analysing Heritage
Making Through a Comparative Study of Humayun's Tomb and Sultan Ghari
(New Delhi)’, and ‘Memory, Material, Museum: Investigating the Exhibition of
Dissonant Histories through Partition’.

I owe my fascination with and commitment to the discipline of history to the Department of History & Archaeology. As an undergraduate student
(2016–2020), I had the opportunity to learn history from some of the best practitioners of the discipline. As someone who had no humanities
background in school, the faculty members, with the utmost care and attention, cultivated in me and many others the critical sensibilities and
dispositions required to acknowledge and appreciate the complexities of the past. The history curriculum at Shiv Nadar IoE is distinct from most
of the undergraduate history programmes offered in India. Students are trained not only in history but also in disciplines such as archaeology and
museum studies. This allowed me to question narrow, artificial chronological labels such ‘ancient’, ‘medieval’ and ‘modern institutionalised by
history departments across India, and think of the past from different vantage points. Disciplines such as archaeology equipped me with tools and
methodologies to visualise and imagine our deep past. Similarly, Museum Studies provided me with an axis to think about categories as broad as
culture, politics, and economy through the politics of objects. It is because of this training that I decided to pursue the discipline for my higher
education. In the end, I would say what I appreciate most about the department is not only the rigour they bring to their teaching but also the
way they cater to the needs of each student when it comes to learning. This is the reason why I decided to come back to the department to pursue
a Ph.D. in history.
